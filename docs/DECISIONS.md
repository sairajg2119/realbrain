# Decisions

Format: what we chose, why, what would change it. Newest last.

## D1: Files in git, no database (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** JSON and JSONL files in three zones (raw, derived, app-facing). **Why:** the brain is about 3 MB, read-only at runtime, edited by one person, and every change should be reviewable and versioned. A database adds cost and a deploy dependency with no benefit. **Revisit when:** user data appears (projects, approved scripts, video jobs) or the retrieval set is too large to ship with the function.

## D2: The app reads only `brain/` (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** one narrow, documented interface (`lib/brain.ts`, `brain/`), fed by export scripts. **Why:** research can change shape freely without breaking the app, and Vercel ships only what the route needs. **Cost:** an export step to keep in sync.

## D3: Raw data is append-only and not in git, protected by a manifest (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** `social-brain/raw/` and `cache/` are gitignored; `MANIFEST.json` (checksums, row counts) is committed; `brain:backup` copies the data to `../realbrain-backups/<date>/`. **Why:** raw captions and transcripts are other people's words (ground rule: permission before sharing), but the data must not be lost. **Known risk:** the Apify datasets are unnamed and expire after the retention period, and the OneDrive folder is the only other copy. Mitigation: run `brain:backup` after data changes and keep the backup folder synced elsewhere. **Revisit:** private object storage if the data grows.

## D4: Performance is relative to the page's own median (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** multiple = plays / the page's median plays, with fixed thresholds and per-page reliability (8+ reels). **Why:** a 50k reel on a small page matters more than 100k on a big one. **Limit:** the window is the latest ~20 reels per page, one day, mostly US.

## D5: Rule-based plus LLM labelling, text only (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** regex flags and a cached Gemini pass (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, `teardown-v1`) over caption and transcript. **Why:** no video was available, and the cache avoids paying twice. **Limit:** labels are imperfect (a few are visibly off). Human spot-check pending. No causal claims are made.

## D6: Script exemplars are curated, not everything (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** 32 reels at 2x or more, at most 4 per hook type, openings of 20+ characters, excluding entertainment and famous-brand pages and humour or emotional-story hooks. **Why:** the model copies what it sees; fame-driven reach teaches the wrong lesson for developers. **Cost:** only 1 Bengaluru reel is in the set.

## D7: Compliance rules and localities stay "unverified" until sourced (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** leave localities `verified: false`; do not present unverified law as verified. **Why:** project notes said rules were checked on 21 Oct, but the repo copy was a draft with no source URLs. **Update (D10):** the rules have now been sourced; localities remain unverified.

## D8: Project docs are tracked, planning docs stay private (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** `.gitignore` uses `docs/*` with explicit un-ignores for the six project documents; `build-brief.md`, `mvp-scope.*` and UI mocks remain private. **Why:** the repo is the source of truth for the product, but the brief and scope contain private planning.

## D9: `CLAUDE.md` lives in `realbrain/` (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** a project-level `CLAUDE.md` that imports `AGENTS.md` (managed by `next dev`). The parent folder's `CLAUDE.md` belongs to a different project (Brewmark) and does not apply here.

## D10: Compliance layer: hand-curated, level-separated, human-gated (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** `realestate-brain/` as a hand-curated store (not scraped), with every rule tagged by level (legal requirement, regulator direction, internal best practice, needs legal review) and status (verified_primary, secondary_reported, unverified), every source in a register, and an escalation process. Tests enforce that unverified rules cannot be called legal requirements and that every rule cites a real source. **Why:** legal text must not be invented or blended with opinion; the brochure alone is not proof. **Cost:** slow to extend; only a human may change a status. **Revisit:** when a lawyer reviews it, record that in the register.

## D11: The RERA number goes on screen, not only in the caption (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** treat K-RERA's 2019 circular as applying to reels as the safe reading, while recording that it is an interpretation. **Why:** the circular covers "any other visual medium" and electronic media and asks for the number top right at half the project-name size. The existing script rule 8 (number in the caption) is necessary under s11(2) but may not be enough. **Implemented 2026-10-22:** `onScreenPlan()` in `lib/compliance.ts` builds the on-screen lines from the verified number; the preview shows them. The real render must burn them in (not built).

## D12: Replaced the draft QR-placement rule (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** `KRERA-QR-PLACEMENT` is now an info note: no Karnataka QR-code requirement was found; QR rules are from Maharashtra and Kerala. **Why:** the draft implied a Karnataka QR rule that the primary source does not contain.

## D13: Only blocked claims stop a script; everything else is shown and acknowledged (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** claims with action `block` (guaranteed returns, pre-launch selling, invented people) become hard failures and get one rewrite; `flag` and `needs_evidence` items appear as 'confirm before you post' and advisor questions, and approval needs a tick. Only blocks and flags can raise a legal question; plain price or amenity mentions do not. **Why:** the scan is pattern matching, so it must not pretend to be a legal ruling or cry wolf on every price. **Cost:** most real scripts carry at least one confirm item (an amenity needs the sanctioned plan), so the tick is common. **Revisit:** when a lawyer reviews the claim classes, or if the tick becomes a habit users click through.

## D14: Property brief before scripts, with citations the code checks (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** an AI call drafts the brief from numbered brochure facts; `lib/brief.ts` keeps only items that cite real fact ids, moves the rest to 'limitations' as removed claims, flags numbers not in the facts, scans wording with the claim scanner, and hands only clean angles to the script writer. Every item shows its basis. **Why:** the brief must not become a second place where facts get invented. **Costs:** one more AI call (about 16 s on `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`); the 40 s timeout means scripts can still be written without it. **Not done, on purpose:** no live web research at request time, no K-RERA lookup, no competitor comparison, no festival or news angles, because none could be verified in the app and the rule is never to present unverified material as fact. Location facts need a human-verified `localities.json` entry; unresearched localities show a research-needed note.

## D15: Briefs are not stored yet (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** return the brief to the browser and keep it for the session. **Why:** the app has no store for user data (D1 revisits at Stage 4), Vercel's filesystem is read-only, and committing a user's project to git is wrong. The brief schema is stable so a store can persist it later, keyed by RERA number. **Cost:** research is repeated per upload, and a refresh loses it.

## D16: Switch Gemini model after two quick 503 retries (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** on 503, retry the same model twice (2 s, 4 s), then move to the next model, and only after the last model fall back to longer waits. **Why:** a live upload took 259 s because each call could wait 4+8+12+16 s on a busy model. **Cost:** a later model may be lower quality. **Not fixed here:** if the first model's quota is exhausted, setting `GEMINI_MODEL` in Vercel to a model with quota left skips the fallback chain entirely.

## D17: Scripts are full, timed beats, and code owns the seconds (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** each script is 6 to 8 beats (hook, build, proof, CTA) with the spoken line, the visual and any overlay text, 30 to 45 seconds. The code times each beat from its words at 2.5 words a second instead of trusting the model's number, and derives hook, body and cta from the beats so every existing check still works. Under 28 s fails as too short, over 60 s as too long; both trigger the one rewrite. Overlay text has the AI label and RERA lines stripped because the app adds them for the whole video. **Why:** the first version returned two or three lines per script, not something a person could shoot, and a model's own timing is a guess. **Cost:** a stricter pipeline and slower generation (more words, and short drafts are rewritten). Visuals are described from renders and plans only; no avatar is described.

## D18: RealBrain chooses the audience for each script (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** the audience picker defaults to 'Best fit'. The model assigns each script an audience (families, first-time buyers, investors, NRI buyers, everyone) with a one-line reason, and is told to use at least two different audiences when the facts support them. A user can still pick one audience. **Why:** you asked for a mix (some for families, some for first-time buyers, some general) decided by what works for the property. **Limit:** an audience the facts barely support (for example investors with no price) can still be chosen; the script's reason is shown so a person can judge. The property brief is now collapsed behind the scripts, because a customer asking for scripts should get scripts first.

## D19: Read big PDFs in the browser and send only the text (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** for a PDF over 4 MB without Blob, or over 20 MB at all, `lib/pdftext.ts` loads pdf.js 3.11.174 from cdnjs and extracts the text on the device; `/api/analyze` accepts `{ text, buyer }`, checks it (`lib/textInput.ts`: at least 400 characters, cut at 400,000) and extracts facts from the text (`extractFactsFromText`). Smaller PDFs keep the existing path. **Why:** the live site has no Blob token, Vercel rejects request bodies over about 4.5 MB, and the model's inline PDF limit is 20 MB, so a normal 26 MB brochure could not reach the app at all. This needs nothing from the owner and works for any size. **Costs:** drawings and images are not read (floor-plan areas come from text only; a banner says so); scanned PDFs fail with a message; a runtime dependency on cdnjs; the brochure text, not the file, is what the server sees. **Revisit:** vendor pdf.js into the app if the CDN becomes a risk; add OCR for scanned files.

## D20: Contradictory brochure figures are kept out of scripts; template fixed (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** limitations the brief marks as contradiction or question are passed to the script writer as figures not to use; the playbook's 'rare feature' template no longer says 'inside a <config>' (patched in `social-brain/scripts/export.ts`, original kept in `backup/`); when RealBrain chooses audiences and all three scripts target the same one, the writer gets one more try asking for a mix; a middle beat over 9 s warns. **Why:** a real brochure test produced 'a FIFA-sized pitch inside your home', a '7-acre clubhouse' the brochure contradicts, and three scripts for one audience, and the first was caused by our own template.

## D21: New visual identity drawn from five reference templates (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** a design language taken from what five Wix real estate and business templates have in common: a full-bleed dark hero with oversized type and little copy, a minimal header with one standout button, high contrast with a single warm accent, geometric grotesque type instead of a serif, split panels, an alternating light band with hairline rules, an angled divider, and a floating call to action. Implemented as: Space Grotesk display type with Inter body; the accent changed from gold to ember orange (#ff6a2b) by changing the existing design tokens, so every screen follows; pill buttons; a new wordmark (light 'Real', bold 'Brain'); and a rebuilt landing page (`components/Landing.tsx`, `app/landing.css`, classes prefixed `lp-`). **Not copied:** no template images, copy, code or assets. The hero uses our own SVG tower illustration; real project photography would replace it and should be the user's own or licensed. **Honest content:** every number on the page is real (487 reels, 25 pages, 25 sourced rules, 3 scripts) and the sample script is labelled as a fictional example. **Limits:** the references were viewed at the browser pane's tablet width only, so desktop spacing is our own judgement; no photography yet; the workspace screens got the new type and accent but not a layout redesign.

## D22: The entry is two cinematic screens with a field of moving houses (2026-10-22)
**Chose:** replace the long landing page of D21 with two screens. Screen 1: 'Hi there, welcome to RealBrain.' and 'Stories that sell home.' over a field of 110 to 260 tiny houses (six elevation styles: gable, modern, villa, apartment, tower, terrace) drifting slowly up and left at different depths, each with its own direction, speed, opacity, scale and sway. Screen 2: 'Let's create something that sells.', 'Turn your property into a story worth watching.' and one button, 'Create my first reel', which sets the existing `stage` to `project` (the real upload workflow: brochure, facts, scripts, approval, video). The house field stays on screen across both; moving forward lifts screen 1 away (blur, rise), eases the houses forward (faster, gentle zoom, slightly dimmer) and raises screen 2 in. Move on with a tap, the Continue arrow, scroll, a swipe up, the down arrow key or the dots; no waiting and no loader. **How it is built:** one canvas (`components/HouseField.tsx`); each house style is drawn once into a sprite at three sizes and stamped with `drawImage`, so 260 houses run at 60 fps in the test browser; layout and motion are pure, tested functions in `lib/houses.ts`; a veil keeps the text readable. Reduced motion draws one still frame and swaps screens with a short fade. **Name:** the app has no sign-in or user profile, so the greeting uses a name only if the `name` prop is given and otherwise says 'Hi there'; no identity is invented. **Tagline:** 'sell home.' is exactly as specified; it is one constant in `components/Landing.tsx` if 'homes' is wanted. **Cost:** the stats strip, sample script and principles sections from D21 are gone from the entry (they remain in git history, commit ed5694d); the ember accent, type and wordmark from D21 stay. **Limits:** reduced-motion behaviour was reviewed in code but not run in a browser with that setting; the house art is original vector shapes, not photography.

## D23: A sturdier AI client, after live uploads failed with 'Something went wrong' (2026-10-22)
**Problem:** on the live site, uploading the 26 MB Nambiar brochure sometimes ran for over a minute and ended in a vague error. The server log of a reproduced failure showed the cause: Gemini's reply had text after its JSON (`Unexpected non-whitespace character after JSON`), the parser took everything from the first `{` to the last `}` and failed, and every retry failed the same way for 3.5 minutes. Other causes of the same symptom were also unhandled: a 500 or 504 from Google, a dropped connection, a call that never returns (there was no time limit), and a short per-minute rate limit being treated like a daily quota (a model rested for 15 minutes). **Chose:** (1) `lib/jsonExtract.ts` takes the first complete JSON object and ignores anything after it; (2) every Gemini call has a time limit (35 to 75 s by kind) and a whole-call budget; (3) `lib/aiPool.ts` rests a failing model and moves to the next, with a per-minute 429 waited out for the seconds Google names and only a daily quota giving up; (4) a bad or cut-off answer is retried, then moves to the next model; (5) extraction and rewrite use low thinking, as the scripts already did; (6) the light model goes first; (7) the property brief is skipped when the first step was already slow (over 45 s), a rewrite is skipped late in a slow request, and the audience retry only runs early, so a request always finishes; (8) errors say what happened (overloaded, too slow, unusable answer, rate limit) with a short reference that is also printed in the server log; (9) the progress screen now says one to two minutes. **Cost:** a slow start produces scripts without the property analysis, and the light model may write a little less well than the larger ones. **Not fixed:** Gemini's free-plan limits and slowness are outside our control; setting `GEMINI_MODEL` in Vercel, or moving to a paid plan, would help most.

## D24. Immersive architectural redesign: Limestone & Bronze, with a real 3D opening
**Chose:** replace the field-of-tiny-houses entry (D22) and the dark ember workspace with one coherent system drawn like an architect's studio. **Opening:** a Three.js scene (`components/ArchScene.tsx`, three 0.170, its own code-split chunk loaded after the page is up) of a modern house built from simple solids: ground-floor glazing with bronze mullions, an upper volume behind timber fins, a stone chimney wall, a cantilevered door canopy, a pool, stepping stones with lamps, instanced trees, distant hills, drifting light motes. A shader sky dome gives the light; a PMREM environment from that sky gives the glass its reflections; ACES tone mapping and soft shadows. A light control switches four moods (morning, golden hour, dusk, night) and the scene blends between them over 1.6 s. The camera drifts in on arrival; the button 'Create my first reel' walks it to the front door while an ivory doorway opens from the centre of the screen into the workspace. The words are exactly as specified ('RealBrain', 'Stories that sell home.'). **Behaviour that matters is pure and tested** (`lib/scene/timeOfDay.ts`, `lib/scene/cameraPath.ts`, 9 tests): light blending, a camera path that only ever moves closer with no jumps, portrait framing. **Workspace:** ivory paper, charcoal ink, bronze for the one thing that matters, hairlines instead of cards, square corners, Cormorant Garamond for ideas and Inter for work (lining figures forced). Upload is a document laid on the table; buyers are a plain list of rooms; scripts are an editorial reading list (a sticky rail with number, checks, copy and Choose; the script set like a screenplay with time in the margin and the spoken line in serif); the approval sheet is paper; the video preview is a dark screening room, the one dark space. **No feature, route or backend change.** **Fallbacks:** a drawn SVG poster stands in while the scene loads and stays if WebGL is missing, data saver is on, or the context is lost; reduced motion draws still frames and shortens the doorway to a fade; the scene is `aria-hidden` and the words, light control (a radio group), dots and button are real controls with keyboard focus. The mobile view keeps the same scene with a wider field of view and a camera pulled back for portrait, a bottom shade for legibility and a full-width button. **Cost:** three.js adds a chunk (loaded only on the opening); the scene is stylised procedural geometry, not photorealistic or scanned. **Not done / not verified:** reduced-motion behaviour reviewed in code but not run with the OS setting; no frame-rate measurement beyond smooth playback in the test browser (Intel Iris Xe); the references interactive3d.in (504) and the ERA project page (seen only through its Vide Infra case study) could not be inspected in full; touch gestures were not tried on a real phone; the dev 'N' badge overlaps the first light button only in development.

## D30. A night tower that draws itself, a four-scene story, and a performance-first homepage
**Root causes of the lag found:** (1) the old homepage autoplayed seven reel videos at once (seven decoders running while scrolling); (2) a full-screen WebGL scene with 2048 shadow maps, 64 instanced trees and 140 particles re-uploaded every frame rendered continuously behind the page; (3) a walkthrough component re-rendered React every 90 ms on a timer; (4) on this computer, OneDrive had about 26 GB of memory committed (the project lives in the OneDrive folder), which starved the machine, hung dev servers and crashed the browsers' graphics process, after which WebGL stayed disabled. **Chose:** a new homepage (`components/landing/Landing.tsx`, `app/landing.css`) with no video, no WebGL and no React state during scroll. The hero is a residential tower at night drawn in generated SVG (`components/landing/TowerArt.tsx`): lines trace, stone faces appear, rooms light from the ground up, then the headline and buttons rise in; it plays once per visit (sessionStorage) and not at all with reduced motion. Pointer parallax moves two depth layers on fine-pointer devices only. Below, one sticky stage and four scenes: the tower close up, a brochure (labelled as an illustration, no invented facts), a script (real lines from a Nambiar run, labelled), and a vertical reel frame (labelled as a concept: avatar video is not connected). The stage follows whichever scene's words are at the middle of the screen. One requestAnimationFrame loop writes styles directly; the scene text reveals with an IntersectionObserver. New tokens (#080A09, #101310, #171B17, #F4F1E9, #A4A69D, #C7A56B), DM Serif Display with Inter, a brain-and-tower logo and favicon (`components/Logo.tsx`, `app/icon.svg`). Workspace steps now read Project, Scripts, Approval, Video; "Everyone" is the stand-alone audience. **Measured:** a scripted full-page scroll over 6 s in the browser pane ran at 60 fps, worst frame 16.8 ms, no long tasks (development build; measured before a later small change to how the stage follows the scenes). **Not done / not verified:** the hero is 2.5D vector art, not a 3D model; it has not been measured on a phone; the 3D house homepage (D27 to D29) is no longer mounted and remains in git history; uncommitted work from another session (a frame-capture script, `public/walk`, a puppeteer dev dependency, edits to `ArchScene.tsx`) was left untouched.
